Kuchipudi dance: the classical dance-drama of Andhra Pradesh

Kuchipudi is one of the eight classical dances of India. It comes from Kuchipudi, a village in the Krishna district of Andhra Pradesh, and carries the village’s name. It began as a dance-drama performed by troupes of men, and today it is danced both as drama and as a solo recital.

Which state does Kuchipudi dance belong to?

Kuchipudi belongs to Andhra Pradesh. It is named after Kuchipudi, a village in Movva mandal of Krishna district, where the families who created the art have lived and taught for centuries.

The village is still the home of the tradition. Descendants of the first performer families live here, the Sri Siddhendra Yogi Kuchipudi Kalapeetham trains dancers here, and Kuchipudi Heritage Arts Society works from here. The nearest city is Vijayawada.

Nritta, nritya and natya: the three strands

Indian classical dance is described in three parts, and Kuchipudi gives full weight to all of them. That balance is what makes it a dance-drama and not only a dance.

  • Nritta is pure dance. There is no story, only rhythm, line and speed. In Kuchipudi it is built from jatis, sequences of steps set to spoken rhythm syllables.
  • Nritya is expressive dance. The dancer interprets the sung words with hand gestures, the eyes and the face. The sabdams of Kuchipudi are danced this way.
  • Natya is drama. The dancer becomes a character, with entrances, dialogue and a story to tell. The Kalapams and the Yakshagana dance-dramas are natya.

What sets Kuchipudi apart

The dancer speaks and sings

Kuchipudi began as theatre, and it has kept the spoken word. A Kuchipudi performer may deliver lines of dialogue or sing a passage while dancing. This is vachika abhinaya, expression through speech, and it is rare among the solo classical dances.

The Tarangam

In the Tarangam the dancer stands on the rim of a brass plate and moves it across the stage in time to the music, with the feet locked in position. Some dancers balance a pot of water on the head as well. The songs come from the Sri Krishna Leela Tarangini of Narayana Tirtha, which gives the item its name.

The entrance song

Each character in a Kuchipudi dance-drama arrives with a pravesa daruvu, a song and dance that introduces who they are. Satyabhama’s entrance in Bhama Kalapam is the most famous, and solo dancers often perform it on its own.

The Sutradhara

The dance-drama is held together by the Sutradhara, the principal narrator. He conducts the performance, links the scenes and talks with the characters. Several of the great gurus of Kuchipudi were known first as Sutradharas.

Stri vesham

For most of its history Kuchipudi was performed only by men, who played the women’s roles too. This art of female impersonation is called stri vesham. Vedantam Satyanarayana Sarma was its most celebrated master in the twentieth century.

A Kuchipudi solo recital, item by item

The solo recital took its present shape from the 1930s. The order varies with the dancer and the occasion, but most recitals move through these stages.

  1. InvocationA prayer in dance, traditionally to Ganesha, that opens the evening.
  2. JatiswaramA piece of nritta. Pure dance set to notes and rhythm, with no words to interpret.
  3. SabdamA narrative piece. The Dasavatara Sabdam, on the ten avatars of Vishnu, is a favourite.
  4. KalapamAn excerpt of natya from a dance-drama, most often Satyabhama’s entrance from Bhama Kalapam.
  5. Padams, javalis and slokasAbhinaya pieces, in which the dancer draws out the meaning of each line of the song.
  6. TarangamThe dance on the brass plate, which traditionally closes the recital.

The dance-dramas of Kuchipudi

The older repertoire of Kuchipudi is a body of dance-dramas played by a full troupe through the night. The best known are:

  • Bhama Kalapam, by Siddhendra Yogi, on the pride, longing and devotion of Satyabhama, the consort of Krishna.
  • Golla Kalapam, a philosophical dialogue between a milkmaid and a Brahmin.
  • Prahlada Charitra, the story of the child devotee Prahlada and his father Hiranyakasipu.
  • Usha Parinayam, the marriage of Usha and Aniruddha.
  • Sasirekha Parinayam, Gayopakhyanam, Rama Natakam, Mohini Rukmangada and Harischandra.

In the twentieth century Vempati Chinna Satyam added a new generation of dance-dramas, among them Ksheera Sagara Mathanam, Sri Krishna Parijatham and Padmavathi Srinivasa Kalyanam.

Costume, make-up and ornaments

A woman dancing Kuchipudi wears a stitched silk costume cut like a sari, with a pleated fan at the front that opens when she sinks into the half-seated stance. Bells are tied at the ankles, a belt sits at the waist, and the hair is dressed in a long plait with flowers and ornaments. Traditional Kuchipudi ornaments were carved from a light wood called boorugu and covered in gold foil.

The plait matters. Satyabhama’s jada is so closely tied to the art that it was painted on the Kuchipudi flag, where each of its ornaments stands for a part of the universe.

Men dancing male roles wear a dhoti. In dance-drama, each character has its own dress and make-up.

Music and instruments

Kuchipudi is danced to Carnatic music. The orchestra sits at the side of the stage and usually includes:

  • a vocalist, who sings the text;
  • a mridangam player, for percussion;
  • a violin, a veena or a flute, for melody;
  • the nattuvanar, who keeps time on small cymbals, speaks the rhythm syllables and conducts the ensemble.

The songs are mainly in Telugu. Sanskrit is used too, as in the Tarangams and the ashtapadis of Jayadeva.

Kuchipudi and Bharatanatyam: how they differ

The two forms share the grammar of the Natya Shastra and both use Carnatic music, so they can look alike at first. These are the differences to watch for.

Questions people ask about Kuchipudi

Which state is Kuchipudi dance from?

Kuchipudi is from Andhra Pradesh. It is named after Kuchipudi village, in Movva mandal of Krishna district, where the dance took shape.

Who is the founder of Kuchipudi dance?

Siddhendra Yogi is honoured as the father of Kuchipudi. He composed Bhama Kalapam, taught it to the Brahmin boys of Kuchipudi village and gave the tradition its form. He is traditionally dated to the 17th century.

Is Kuchipudi a classical dance?

Yes. Kuchipudi is one of the eight classical dances of India recognised by the Sangeet Natak Akademi. The others are Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, Odissi and Sattriya.

What is the plate dance in Kuchipudi called?

It is called the Tarangam. The dancer stands on the rim of a brass plate and moves it in time to the music, sometimes with a pot of water balanced on the head.

What is Bhama Kalapam?

Bhama Kalapam is the founding dance-drama of Kuchipudi, composed by Siddhendra Yogi. It tells of Satyabhama, the proud consort of Krishna, and her longing for him when they are apart.

What is the difference between Kuchipudi and Bharatanatyam?

Kuchipudi comes from Andhra Pradesh and grew out of dance-drama, so its dancers may speak and sing, and it includes the Tarangam on a brass plate. Bharatanatyam comes from Tamil Nadu and grew out of solo temple and court dance, and its dancers do not speak.

Can women perform Kuchipudi?

Yes. For most of its history Kuchipudi was performed only by men, who played the women's roles too. In the twentieth century gurus such as Vedantam Lakshminarayana Sastry began to teach women, and women now dance Kuchipudi all over the world.

What language are Kuchipudi songs in?

Mainly Telugu, with some Sanskrit. The music is Carnatic.

Where is Kuchipudi village?

Kuchipudi village is in Movva mandal of Krishna district, Andhra Pradesh, PIN 521136. The nearest city is Vijayawada.
