What is Kuchipudi?

Kuchipudi is a classical dance of India from the village of Kuchipudi in Andhra Pradesh. It joins pure dance, expressive dance and drama, and its dancers may also speak and sing. Its best-known item is the Tarangam, danced on the rim of a brass plate.

Nritta
Pure dance. Rhythm and line for their own sake, built from jatis, the rhythmic sequences.
Nritya
Expressive dance. Hands, eyes and face carry the meaning of the sung words, as in the sabdams.
Natya
Drama. Characters, story and speech, as in Bhama Kalapam and the other dance-dramas.

A short history of Kuchipudi

From a village in the Krishna delta to stages around the world, in seven moments.

  1. 17th century

    Siddhendra Yogi composes Bhama Kalapam and teaches it to the Brahmin boys of Kuchipudi village.

  2. Late 1600s

    Tradition holds that Abul Hasan Tana Shah, the ruler of Golconda, grants the village to the families who practise the art.

  3. Early 1900s

    Troupes such as Chinta Venkataramayya's Venkatarama Natya Mandali tour dance-dramas across the Telugu country.

  4. 1930s

    Vedantam Lakshminarayana Sastry opens the dance-drama tradition to solo performance and teaches women.

  5. 1963

    Vempati Chinna Satyam founds the Kuchipudi Art Academy in Madras, and Kuchipudi reaches the concert stage worldwide.

  6. 1974

    Vedantam Parvateesam creates the Kuchipudi flag, the patakam.

  7. 2024

    The Society marks fifty years of the flag with the three-day Kuchipudi Pathaka Swarnotsavalu in Kuchipudi village.
