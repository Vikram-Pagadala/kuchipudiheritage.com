The flag of Kuchipudi

The Kuchipudi flag, called the patakam, is a four-sided cloth banner in light pink. A stalk of sugarcane serves as its pole, and the Satyabhama jada, the heroine's plait, is painted on the cloth. It was created in 1974.

A flag is more than a piece of cloth. It carries the history, culture and values of the people who raise it, and it gives them a single thing to gather around. The Kuchipudi patakam does this for a dance. It stands for the idea that a tradition with many families, styles and voices is still one art.

The flag was designed by the hereditary guru Vedantam Parvateesam, a teacher, writer and founding member of Siddhendra Kalakshetram in Kuchipudi village.

The Satyabhama jada, ornament by ornament

The plait painted on the flag is the one worn by Satyabhama in Bhama Kalapam, the founding dance-drama of Kuchipudi. In the play she is proud and beautiful, and her long plait is her emblem. On the flag it becomes a picture of the cosmos, read from the top down.

  • The parrot at the top stands for the ego. Pride, too, is treated as a facet of the divine.
  • Adi Sesha, the great serpent, stands for intelligence and wisdom.
  • The Trimurtis, Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, are shown, along with the navaratnas, the nine precious gems.
  • Twenty-seven stars stand for the 27 nakshatras of Hindu astrology.
  • Three large balls at the end of the plait stand for the three worlds, and the nine planets are shown with them. Some read the twelve balls as the twelve signs of the zodiac.

There are two kinds of jada, a smaller and a larger. The larger has 108 petals. Either way the plait stands for feminine beauty and, through it, for the universe, nature and the divine.

What the flag stands for

Taken together, the parts of the patakam say three things.

The first is unity in diversity. The flag gathers different elements into one design, as Kuchipudi gathers different families and schools into one tradition, and it reminds dancers that art can bring people together across boundaries.

The second is the worth of the individual artist. The flag honours each dancer's identity and expression, and it encourages creativity and personal growth.

The third is the sovereignty of the artist: the freedom to create and to express oneself without bounds.

Fifty years of the flag: Kuchipudi Pathaka Swarnotsavalu

The flag reached its fiftieth year in 2024. Kuchipudi Heritage Arts Society marked the golden jubilee with the Kuchipudi Natya Pathaka Swarnotsavalu, an international gathering of dancers held in Kuchipudi village from 27 to 29 December 2024.

Over three days there were lectures by senior gurus and scholars, a photo exhibition on the Kuchipudi dance-dramas, tributes to veterans of the art, and performances by artists and groups from across India and from abroad. A 50-foot stupa was built in the village, and the Kuchipudi flag was hoisted on it.

The jubilee was also marked in Washington, D.C., in a programme presented with the Embassy of India and Kuchipudi Kala Nilayam.
